# Projection structural seam decision — 2026-09-25

Status: architecture recommendation, not implementation or a throughput claim. Source: merged `56b89fda7df2e07c321a7d357532641a0fe309d7` (PR #359). No new load or infrastructure was run for this memo.

## Decision

Split by **operational read contract versus audit timeline**, rather than treating the existing `status/fill` and `timeline/payload` SQL functions as independently safe workers. Put command results, orders, executions, trades, and complete own-order lifecycle in one ordered operational projection. Put immutable timeline headers and payload access in an independently checkpointed audit projection. Market state remains a downstream operational projection with its own freshness proof. First make lifecycle derivable from compact canonical outcome facts without reading `runtime_events`; then separate audit persistence. Keep the present full transaction as the rollback path until exact business and replay parity passes.

This is a capacity hypothesis. Running two workers against the same PostgreSQL instance **does not remove rows or WAL**. The likely capacity mechanisms are (a) removing audit writes and indexes from the operational commit's critical path, (b) avoiding repeated lifecycle scans of historical executions and events, (c) reducing duplicated audit payload storage where canonical facts are sufficient, and (d) only if needed, placing the audit projection on a separately provisioned PostgreSQL instance. Each must earn its own matched full-pipeline result. Do not claim 10k from stage-only or postdrain completion.

## Evidence boundary and present path

The historical sustained full-projection pass is H4/C4 at about 2,500 accepted commands/s for 300s. C5/H1 prove approximately 10,000/s for venue-core materialization **with projections disabled**, not the full pipeline. On the merged C43 baseline, the fresh c-32, six-materializer, 16-projector-owner, 10k-offered, 300s fixture accepted/direct-acked/materialized 2,992,504 (9,973.99/s). A **later** projector collection counted 2,967,975 (9,892.24/s) and 26,029 lag. Those snapshots are not simultaneous; the frozen full-pipeline checker failed, including downstream freshness/cohort authority, and 20% drain margin is unproven. After drain, source and projection both reached 2,992,504, all 16 frontiers were contiguous, queues emptied, and rebuilds matched 2,465,524 lifecycle and 64 market rows. C42 also failed at 9,885.93/s projected with 34,201 lag. C43's index ablation lowered projection WAL/accepted from C42's 5,820.59 to 5,658.69 bytes (about 2.8%); its 6.31/s projector-rate difference does not establish a throughput gain because intake and sampler timing differed. See [C42–C43 ledger](../THROUGHPUT_BASELINES.md#c42--lock-only-dirty-conflicts-write-target-removed-full-pipeline-timed-fail) and the [active plan](../PROJECTION_THROUGHPUT_SCALING_PLAN.md#september-25-canonical-batch-insert-diagnostic).

Current path, verified in source:

```text
boundary -> durable command acknowledgement (202 only after ingress ack)
  -> deterministic venue/session/instrument lane in Go engine
  -> durable VenueEventBatch -> compact canonical Postgres materializer commit
  -> per-partition canonical_command_outcomes and immutable result/batch facts
  -> 16 projection owners select contiguous partition-sequence members
  -> batch claim + normalized SQL + watermark completion in one transaction
  -> dirty order queue -> lifecycle state -> dirty instrument queue -> market snapshot
  -> API: canonical command lookup; projected own orders/fills/trades/events/market
```

Same-store `runtime_project_canonical_command_outcome_members` claims exact ordered members and invokes `runtime_persist_submit_outcomes`; separated-store Kotlin does the equivalent within one projection transaction. The full SQL wrapper calls status/fill then timeline/payload serially before watermark advancement. Status/fill writes `submit_results`, `orders`, `executions`, `trades`, and lifecycle invalidations. Timeline writes `runtime_events` and `runtime_event_payloads`; deterministic events use stream sequence and ordinal, while legacy events use the trace allocator. Lifecycle refresh subsequently aggregates executions and events for each dirty order; it uses `OrderModified`, `OrderCancelled`, and `OrderRejected` timeline facts. Market refresh reads lifecycle state. Thus today's `command-status` ablation is **not** an independent own-order-fresh gate. Sources: [claim/membership SQL](../../scripts/dev/db/migrations/runtime/0050_projection_batch_claims.sql), [full wrapper](../../scripts/dev/db/migrations/runtime/0041_deterministic_timeline_projection_sequence.sql), [active status/fill](../../scripts/dev/db/migrations/runtime/0059_trade_replay_and_parse.sql), [timeline/payload](../../scripts/dev/db/migrations/runtime/0043_runtime_event_payload_cold_table.sql), [lifecycle refresh](../../scripts/dev/db/migrations/runtime/0052_projection_dirty_serialization.sql), and [Kotlin separated-store flow](../../services/platform-runtime/src/main/kotlin/com/reef/platform/infrastructure/persistence/PostgresRuntimePersistence.kt).

C30's nested diagnostic measured 6,624 full persistence calls at 604.5ms mean; its status/fill statement averaged 357.8ms and 8.93GB tracked WAL, timeline/payload 144.7ms and 6.62GB WAL, and the separate submit-result conflict check 43.0ms. Parent/child times overlap and cannot be added or interpreted as a wall-clock saving from a split. The aged C32 stage-only probe processed synthetic mixed outcomes at about 53k/s with 16 writers and 2.36–2.37KB WAL/outcome, without timeline, lifecycle/market workers, or live contention. It disproves a simple claim that isolated status/fill SQL alone has a measured 10k capacity ceiling. C42 still recorded about 19.96 million projection inserts and 17.47GB projection WAL despite eliminating dirty-queue tuple updates. The stronger observed problem is concurrent full-pipeline write amplification and freshness residence, not one demonstrably slow table. [C30 raw aggregate](../evidence/projection-canonical-sql-c30-2026-09-25.json), [aged stage probe](PROJECTION_STATUS_FILL_AGED_PROFILE_2026-09-25.md), [C28 same-prefix timing](../THROUGHPUT_BASELINES.md#c28--same-prefix-marker-timing-full-projection-10k-diagnostic).

## Ranked structural options

| Rank | Option | Why / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Operational read model plus independently checkpointed audit model, with compact lifecycle facts from canonical outcomes | Separates actual read/freshness ownership and removes audit commits from operational latency. Required dependency repair makes the split correct. **Choose, staged.** Extra stores alone do not reduce total write work. |
| 2 | Thin audit event locator with payload reconstructed from canonical facts | Potentially removes one full JSON copy and payload-table insert per event; could beat physical splitting for total WAL. **Conditional next slice**, only after proving canonical data and retention are self-contained for every public timeline/read/replay case, including legacy batches and trace metadata. No measured savings yet. |
| 3 | Per-order incremental lifecycle maintenance only | Avoids repeated historical execution/event aggregation; C25 shows recompute/write, not dirty claims, dominates isolated lifecycle time. Could increase hot-row updates and lock contention. Trial only with exact parity and full-system gate. |
| 4 | Run current status/fill and timeline functions as separate workers/checkpoints | Removes serial wait but preserves all writes, and current lifecycle reads timeline. Unsafe as an own-order-fresh claim until the dependency is replaced or a join barrier is enforced. |
| 5 | More owners, index drops, or dirty-queue rewrites | C31 rejected an unused-index drop; C41–C43 and C25 show these bounded changes did not clear full-pipeline capacity. Retain valid cleanups, but do not lead with another such treatment. |

No option has a supported numerical 10k or 12k capacity forecast. C30's 8.93/6.62GB WAL attribution describes *where* writes occurred, not removable bytes. Removing audit payload duplication has a calculable upper bound only after measuring how much of the 6.62GB is actual payload versus headers, indexes, and transaction overhead.

## Target contract and failure behavior

**Authority and keys.** Canonical `VenueEventBatch` and `canonical_command_outcomes` remain immutable replay/audit authority; no new work goes onto the durable acceptance path. Keep the existing event-stream + partition-ID + contiguous `stream_sequence` membership and batch payload hash. Within each partition, apply effects in sequence order. Use the same venue-session/instrument lane as matching where metadata exists. Coalesce all order effects within a batch, but never reorder a given order's commands/fills. A maker/taker fill affecting an older order must use the canonical event's actual order IDs, exact numeric quantities, and deterministic event ordinal. Validate that both affected orders map to the expected lane; if not, introduce an explicit order-key sequencer/merge rule and test it before incremental maintenance. Do not infer order causality from timestamp or UUID sort order.

**Operational transaction.** For a claimed canonical member set, write command result, accepted order, executions, trades, and compact per-order lifecycle transition/accumulator in one PostgreSQL transaction; advance `operational` partition watermarks and complete the stage-specific D-055 claim only after those effects commit. Apply one order-state update per affected order per batch where parity allows, carrying original/current quantity, exact cumulative fill, latest modification, terminal flags, and last-event key. Preserve current text and numeric shadow-field semantics and complete terminal zero remainder. Feed market invalidations in that same transaction; market snapshot/level maintenance has its own frontier and cannot claim coverage until affected operational transactions and market invalidations commit. Initially keep market's existing reference SQL for parity; do not introduce a book-delta algorithm in the same slice.

**Audit transaction.** Independently claim the same canonical membership under `audit` stage identity. Insert event header and payload (or later a proven locator) and advance `audit` per-partition watermark atomically. `event_id` uniqueness must mean *identical semantic content*, not merely an occupied key. Current timeline `ON CONFLICT (event_id) DO NOTHING` for both tables can silently ignore a different incoming header or payload under the same ID; source inspection establishes this possibility, **not observed corruption**. Before parallelization, compare all semantic header fields, deterministic sequence, typed modify facts, and full payload digest/bytes on conflict; identical replay is a no-op, mismatch raises a permanent conflict and leaves the stage frontier unchanged. Event header without required payload or vice versa fails closed, except explicitly migrated legacy inline-payload rows with a verified equivalence rule. Preserve deterministic trace ordering and legacy allocator behavior, or migrate legacy data with a proven mapping. Add event identity/version to the existing batch claim contract rather than repurposing a `full` identity.

**Frontiers and reads.** Store `projection_epoch`, stage, event stream, partition, and last contiguous sequence; represent absent partitions and errors explicitly. A public command lookup continues to prefer canonical command outcomes, so it does not wait for audit. Route own-order, fill, trade, and current-market reads to the operational store; route order/trace timeline reads to audit. Any response combining lifecycle and timeline facts is covered only through the **componentwise minimum** of operational and audit frontiers for its source partitions. Never combine a newer order state with an older event trail while labelling the whole response fresh. Return the existing source/lag metadata and apply the current route's freshness/error behavior; do not silently change a public route or promise stronger consistency than the old one. Cross-database reads do not share an MVCC snapshot, so capture/verify frontier tokens around a combined read or keep it on the legacy store until an as-of read contract exists. Market freshness additionally needs a committed operational prefix and a drained/covered market queue; an empty unlogged queue alone is not proof after restart.

**Backpressure and recovery.** Preserve `control-room-fresh` versus `venue-core` policy. Control-room freshness samples both stage lags plus lifecycle/market coverage; venue-core continues to report projection lag while gating durable worker lag. In either mode, reject *before* durable ingress if finite canonical retention, disk, or replay horizon would be exceeded; never emit 202 until durable acceptance itself is acknowledged. A stalled audit stage is an explicit full-pipeline failure, not permission to call operational-only throughput “full.” On crash, recover each stage from its last committed contiguous canonical frontier and D-055 claim; ambiguous commits must read completed claims rather than reapply effects. Rebuild unlogged dirty queues from canonical/frontier gaps after PostgreSQL restart and invalidate freshness proofs across postmaster generations until verified. Permanent hash/content mismatches quarantine the member, alert, and stop that partition's frontier; no skip, overwrite, or dead-letter-and-advance. Transient serialization/deadlock retries remain bounded by the immutable retry deadline. Failed projection writes do not undo canonical acceptance; they create visible lag and backpressure.

**Placement and rollout.** Keep canonical tables in canonical Postgres. Begin with operational and audit schemas/claims/frontiers in the existing projection instance to prove semantics. A separate audit PostgreSQL **instance**, with its own WAL/storage budget, is a later topology experiment; a second database in the same instance shares core I/O resources. Preserve local Compose single-instance support. Build a shadow operational projection from canonical facts, compare full rows and frontiers, then switch individual API read adapters only after parity. Build audit shadow independently. Retain the old full projector and read routing through the rollback window, but do not run synchronous dual writes on intake. Rollback is a routing/worker switch to the old projection if its frontier is current; otherwise replay the old projection from canonical authority before declaring rollback complete. Version the projection epoch and migrations; do not rewind a watermark in place or reuse a stage name for different effects. Only retire old tables after a separate retention/rebuild decision.

## Small implementation slices and one decisive gate each

Use the C43 fixture, source commit/image manifest, six materializers, 16 owners, 64 instruments, 300s/10k offered, database settings, observer cadence, and fixed collection points as the comparison template. Record the actual read-load setting and any unavoidable code/topology/hardware change explicitly; preserve failed setup and runs under new ledger IDs. The C43 raw artifact path is host-local and may be unavailable; the ledger and hashes remain the authority. Do not depend on the test droplet. For every material slice, run focused fault/parity tests first, then **one matched full-pipeline treatment against a fresh control**, using the frozen checker and original artifacts; do not add a broad profiling campaign.

1. **Conflict guard and stage identity.** Add equal-content event/payload replay checks, stage/epoch identity, and contiguous-frontier tests without changing full-path routing. Test same-ID same-content retry, changed header/payload/typed fact, missing payload, ambiguous commit, and rollback-before-watermark. Decisive gate: fresh C43-shaped full run versus fresh unchanged control, requiring exact counts, business/replay equality, no conflict false positives, and no material regression in the frozen timed/freshness measures. A failure keeps the old full path.
2. **Compact operational lifecycle derivation.** Implement canonical-derived per-order transitions behind a shadow projection; compare every lifecycle business field (including numeric shadows) to a quiesced full rebuild for submit/reject/cancel/modify/partial fill/trade replay, interleaved maker/taker fills, conflicting retries, and restart. Decisive gate: one fresh matched 10k/300s full-pipeline run with the same audit path, requiring the frozen 5s lifecycle, 10s market, and 30s tail bounds, exact frontiers/reference, and an improved operational residence/cost profile. If per-order update contention or WAL rises without better full-pipeline freshness, revert this slice.
3. **Independent audit checkpoint and worker.** Keep existing event/payload rows initially; move audit writes out of the operational transaction. Test ahead/behind stages, event conflict quarantine, crash between commits, mixed API reads, and independent replay. Decisive gate: matched 10k/300s full-pipeline run counting both frontiers at fixed collection points and requiring **both** audit and operational exact freshness, lifecycle/market bounds, zero gaps, and full rebuild parity. An operational-only pass is diagnostic, not promotion.
4. **Audit storage reduction, then placement only if necessary.** Prove canonical payload/trace self-containment and replace duplicate payload rows with an indexed locator or a bounded archive fetch behind unchanged read semantics; keep immutable event identity and retention. Test old/new payload byte parity, trace/order pagination and ordering, archive expiry, corruption, and point-read latency. Decisive gate: one fresh matched full-pipeline 10k/300s comparison showing lower audit rows/WAL/bytes per accepted command **and** unchanged read/freshness/replay results. If still below target, test a separate audit instance under a stated fixed total CPU/RAM/I/O budget; its one decisive matched gate is the same full-pipeline run, explicitly attributing the topology/resource change rather than claiming software efficiency.

Promotion remains the repository's frozen exact-cohort full-pipeline gate, not a revised threshold: accepted/direct-acked/materialized and **both** projected stage counts/frontiers match at the prescribed measurement boundary; no failures, retries, deadlocks, sequence holes, or business/replay mismatch; lifecycle/market/timeline coverage and sampler authority pass; and at least 20% measured drain headroom is demonstrated with the established drain method, including aged state and read load required by the plan. A measured drain slope must be distinguished from a conservative coverage bound and from postdrain equality. If an independent audit stage cannot meet the same exact freshness requirement, the architecture has not met the stated goal.

## Assumptions and open decisions

- The canonical outcome plus retained command metadata can reproduce every venue-generated event's payload and trace fields. This must be proved before audit locator storage or shorter retention; older compatibility batches may require immutable backfill.
- Current per-instrument lane ownership is sufficient for both sides of each fill. Verify actual cross-partition order touches; if present, specify deterministic order-key sequencing before incremental lifecycle.
- The current `runtime_events`/payload tables are rebuildable read projections for venue outcomes; post-trade canonical event history and public audit requirements may have different ownership and must not be pruned by this plan.
- C43's full-pipeline gap is small in *rate* but has no proven 20% reserve; a merely 0.1–1% improvement is not the target. The exact point where PostgreSQL write/WAL contention or lifecycle recompute becomes the limiter after separation is unknown.
- Decide the retention/replay horizon, combined-read consistency token, audit-instance resource budget, and measured read-latency SLO before cutting over. If those choices cannot preserve current API behavior, keep the legacy full read path while still measuring the shadow projection.
