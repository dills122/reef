# Ordered post-match capacity: institutional practice and Reef decision gate

Status: research recommendation for PM10 lead, 2026-09-28. No architecture decision, product change, public-read qualification, or throughput pass is made here. Decision owner: PM10 integration lead and D-059 owner.

## Decision in one page

**Question.** Which public institutional and ledger designs can help Reef sustain 10,000 accepted commands/s while preserving its retained admission rank, same-account and source-partition predecessors, scarce-balance winner, exact four-leg DvP, deterministic replay, and live read freshness?

**Recommendation (inference).** Prototype a bounded, rank-ordered settlement executor that amortizes proof and persistence across consecutive eligible windows, while retaining each window's identity, result, four postings or typed break, checkpoint, and completion proof. Commit the whole execution group in one transaction, then expose no partial group result. Allocate ranks in a separate bounded *atomic* admission transaction that commits all rank and dependency facts with the counter advance. Run independent source-partition live projection workers with bulk append-only facts and transactionally coupled checkpoints. Keep disjoint account components parallel. Amend D-059 and its transition contract before shipping grouped commits because D-059 currently requires one settlement transaction per window. This is a test candidate, not a proven 10k design.

**Why now (observation).** Corrected PM-S3 treatment accepted/direct-acked 2,776,548 commands over 300 seconds (9,254.07/s), but in-load table-statistics samples showed about 2,018–2,215 settlement intake trades/s and only 75.6–96.3 transition attempts/s. Live receipts and trade facts also lagged intake by roughly 3:1 at a later diagnostic snapshot. Exact closed-cohort settlement checks did not finish. The separate local, fixed-admission-log grouped prototype reported 604–696 trades/s versus 417–518 with single-window execution; its fixture needs about 4,400 trades/s at a 0.44 trade/outcome mix. These are different environments and stages, not a causal speedup or capacity comparison. See [capacity decision](POST_MATCH_SETTLEMENT_CAPACITY_DECISION_2026-09-27.md), [PM-S3 fault analysis](POST_MATCH_PM_S3_FAULT_LINE_ANALYSIS_2026-09-28.md), and [PM10 execution index](../work/POSTMATCH_10K_PARALLEL_EXECUTION_INDEX_2026-09-28.md), included in this documentation branch. D-059 is [accepted here](../DECISIONS.md#d-059-durable-settlement-admission-order-for-scarce-accounts).

**Scope and stop condition.** Review public primary standards, FMI disclosures, implementer documentation, and peer-reviewed systems papers; compare three designs; define a falsifiable local proof. No benchmark, implementation, PR, deployment, or decision amendment in this research lane. External throughput numbers are never converted into a Reef estimate.

## Source hierarchy and transfer limits

1. **Normative settlement semantics.** CPMI-IOSCO's April 2012 [PFMI](https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d101a.pdf), Principles 8 and 12, requires a clearly defined finality point and linked final settlement of both sides of an exchange. PFMI permits gross or net designs; it does **not** prescribe Reef's four ledger legs, durable rank, or scarce-balance winner. Those are Reef's stricter accepted contract.
2. **Documented institutional operating models.** DTCC/DTC/NSCC and ECB/T2S describe settlement mechanics and risk controls publicly. Their matching engines, account arbitration algorithms, database transaction boundaries, and peak per-second internal throughput are largely private. Do not infer those details from product descriptions.
3. **Implementer and platform mechanics.** TigerBeetle, Modern Treasury, PostgreSQL/pgJDBC, and NYSE/AWS disclose useful batching, conditional-balance, and streaming patterns. Vendor measurements or claims are self-reported and use other workloads.
4. **Research analogy.** Calvin (SIGMOD 2012) and FoundationDB (SIGMOD 2021) show deterministic input scheduling and batched commit-version allocation. Their mechanisms are analogies, not proof that Reef's four-leg PostgreSQL workload reaches target.

**Surprising boundary.** DTCC's netted DvP combines intraday securities movements, end-of-day funds settlement and institutional risk resources; DTC can pend an instruction that fails a resource check. This can still satisfy its own DvP/legal framework, but neither a pending instruction nor net end-of-day cash is equivalent to Reef deciding each ranked trade now. Similarly, TigerBeetle's batch commit allows mixed per-event success; an atomic SQL group should preserve each window's individual settle/break outcome instead of turning one failed trade into an all-trades failure. ## Options under one acceptance contract

| Design | Admission and execution | Audit, failure and read model | Capacity opportunity and limit | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Ranked group executor with bulk facts** | One transaction allocates a bounded contiguous rank range and commits each window's source identity/digest, account membership, predecessor links, dependency digest and frontier. A separate group transaction selects rank-sorted windows only if all external predecessors have completed (or earlier selected windows satisfy them), locks union accounts in stable order, evaluates balances **window by window**, then persists every attempt, exact four legs or typed break, account checkpoints, completion proofs and frontiers. | Crash before group commit leaves none applied; ambiguous commit must be recognized by durable group and per-window identities. Rebuild imports the same admission log and verifies source/policy/account/dependency/opening and ledger facts. Live projection independently consumes canonical effects and commits facts plus per-partition checkpoint. | Amortizes rank-counter and proof round trips plus fact writes. Hot chain still has a serial decision loop and WAL/index cost. At this research freeze, the local group-of-four diagnostic at 604–696 trades/s left roughly 6.3–7.3× gap to 4,400/s; later prototype measurements are tracked in the execution index. | **Prototype first.** Requires D-059 amendment before shipping multi-window execution commit. |
| **B. Netting or deferred settlement** | Aggregate offsetting obligations by member/security or cash account; settle net positions in later cycle, possibly with collateral and a pending queue. | Needs a new finality point, exposure controls, per-trade provenance-to-net mapping, default handling and different public status semantics. Simple trade-level four-leg facts no longer directly describe immediate settlement. | Can dramatically reduce physical settlement movements in institutional systems, but netting ratio, legal model and throughput benefit for Reef unknown. | **Out of current contract.** Only reconsider with explicit product/risk decision and proof that every ranked scarce-balance winner and DvP result is preserved or intentionally changed. |
| **C. Account-partitioned ledger** | Partition non-overlapping account sets and execute independent components in parallel. Multi-account trades need durable total arbitration or a deterministic cross-partition protocol; a global common cash account belongs to every such trade's dependency component. | Each shard needs ordered durable input, atomic cross-shard four-leg outcome, retry/idempotency and merged causal read frontier. An external ledger adds cross-store authority/recovery protocol. | Scales diverse-account workloads. Sharding the same conditional hot account cannot make its winner decisions independent; more partitions/threads may increase contention. | **Keep as parallel complement**, not a replacement for the hot chain. Explore engine migration only after measured SQL/lock budget and transactional design. |

**Admission detail.** A database sequence, wall clock, or distributed timestamp is not a retained rank/dependency log by itself. An allocation transaction must not commit a rank reservation separately from window memberships and predecessors. If a range transaction aborts, no new rank or frontier may be visible. Within a range, compute predecessor edges in rank order, including earlier windows in that range. The single global counter remains locked until the admission transaction commits every rank fact. Precompute source/account proofs before locking and use set-based inserts to shorten that transaction; bounded ranges amortize its unavoidable lock hold per window. Measure whether one counter still caps rank/s.

**Execution detail.** Selection must not jump over a blocked predecessor on any affected account or source partition. A larger group can include the blocked successor only after its predecessor is earlier in the same group or already completed. Lock ordering avoids deadlock but never defines scarce-account winners. Bound groups by window count, trades, bytes, lock hold and transaction duration. A slow or invalid window cannot leave a partly committed chain; persist a typed break at its own rank where policy permits, or abort/retry the group.

**Live projection detail.** Source partitions are independent lanes only where identities and ownership permit. Advance each contiguous source checkpoint in the same transaction as execution/trade/order/market facts; reject gaps and semantic conflicts. Batch append-only receipt/trade/execution inserts, then profile mutable order-state upserts separately. Show a combined API view only when all causal source frontiers it needs are covered. A high downstream count or empty queue alone does not prove current business state. NYSE's feed publication supports separation of dissemination, not this exact checkpoint algorithm.

## Falsifiable next gate for lead

1. **Freeze one source cohort and admission log.** Compare baseline and candidate execution on identical rank/source/account/dependency facts. Run five-actor hot-account and diverse-account cohorts. Report source outcome and trade mix, number and longest depth of account dependencies, window size distribution, and rejected/broken trades.
2. **Prove semantics before rate.** Fault before and after admission commit and grouped execution commit; retry ambiguous commits; import/replay the same log. Check rank contiguity, predecessor closure, exact per-rank decision, four-leg amounts/currency, account conservation, checkpoint/frontier and fact hashes. Verify a scarce-balance fixture where reversing two competing ranks changes the winner. Demonstrate disjoint components can progress independently.
3. **Measure each critical section.** Record rank-counter wait/hold/transaction time; predecessor-complete-to-start delay; planning, union-lock, ordered balance evaluation, fact persistence and commit time; windows and trades per commit; SQL calls, WAL bytes/trade, index and table growth, retries/deadlocks. Compare groups 1/4/8/16/32 with byte and time caps, not only group count.
4. **Measure live separately and together.** Capture source-read, decode/plan, append-only writes, order-state upserts, commit, and per-partition in-load frontier/lag. Verify rewrite-on/off statement counts and exact replay parity. A writer-only pass is not an API-freshness pass.
5. **Capacity gate.** At the observed ~0.44 trades/outcome mix, 10,000 outcomes/s implies ~4,400 trades/s and, around 40 trades/window, ~110 completed windows/s. A 16-window all-hot chain therefore needs about 145 ms/group *including* its ordered decisions and writes; this is arithmetic design budget, not observed latency. Require sustained in-load no-growing mandatory backlog, exact stopped-source checks, bounded drain and freshness under the active plan. Use matched control/treatment host, source, topology, PostgreSQL settings, observer and workload; finish exact checkers inside the two-hour hosted budget. Measure local full-size checker time before booking a disposable run.

**What would change recommendation.** Reject A as sufficient if grouped execution cannot reach the required trade/s with headroom on fixed hot-account log, or if group commit duration breaks freshness despite high aggregate rate. Prioritize fact-storage redesign if persistence remains dominant (the 24-window local hot-group diagnostic available at this research freeze spent 907.8 of 1,582.8 ms persisting facts); prioritize readiness scheduling if predecessor-complete-to-start dominates. If a representative diverse-account workload meets target via partition parallelism while hot fixture is unrepresentative, change workload target and re-evaluate C. Consider netting only after a signed semantic/finality/risk change; it cannot be selected as a transparent speed optimization.

## Evidence quality and unknowns

**High confidence:** PFMI obligations, public product mechanics, TigerBeetle request semantics, pgJDBC default, and Reef's recorded PM-S3 count/backlog direction. **Medium confidence:** hot-account dependency is near one chain in this fixture; the longest 27,617/27,622 depth came from an earlier invalid 200-connection treatment, not the corrected 320/240 run. **Low/unknown:** exact PM-S3 per-stage wall time, cause of 9,254/s accepted rate, integrated gain from rank range/group/COPY, live writer bottleneck split, settlement replay behavior of an unbuilt group, representative account skew, and public internal throughput of DTCC/T2S/NYSE. PM-S3 SQL times sum concurrent calls and must not be read as elapsed path latency. The local 604–696 trades/s grouped result is diagnostic, not a hosted or integrated gate.

No external system in this review publishes an implementation of **Reef's exact** durable cross-partition rank, instant scarce-account arbitration, per-trade four-leg DvP, and live projection contract. Lead must own the D-059 amendment and acceptance after local proof; this paper supplies constraints, candidate and falsifiers only.
