# Calcify capacity lane: one decision per booked trade (combined capture and settle)

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: **adopted by the owner on 2026-10-15 as the opt-in decision mode `capture-settle-v1` (variant LEAN, OFF by default); the implementation and its real-stack measurements are described in CALCIFY_CAPACITY_PROFILE.md decision 7 and the throughput ledger entry CAL-FIN-P25 in docs/THROUGHPUT_BASELINES.md. Below is the original investigation, kept as written (it was done against the code before the flat state trie and the removed re-encoding; the prototype test sources were ported into the product tests rather than merged). Two refinements in the adopted version: the attempt budget is checked only for a trade that would write an attempt leaf, and the mode is bound through the capacity configuration digest.** Original status: investigation with a research prototype and measurements; a contract-change proposal, not adopted, not the default, not wired into the launcher. Nothing here changes the finite P3 path or any shipped behaviour. One laptop (Apple M1 Max, shared with three other benchmark sessions), JDK 21, no RocksDB, no Kafka client, no real stack run for the combined flow; no capacity claim.

## The question
In the capacity profile's instant settlement mode the decoder turns each executed trade into two ledger inputs, `Capture` and the first `Settle` (`CapacityResolvedContextDecoder`, `CapacityInputDecoders`), and the lane decides each one: two DECISION records, two state-commitment roots, two chain links, two decisions-topic records per trade. Would one decision that books capture and first settlement together be worth a contract change? The hypothesis in the lane-cost note was "about 1.5 to 1.8 times if the decision count is halved", unmeasured.

## Answer in one paragraph
Measured per booked trade on one lane (no broker): the combined decision costs **1.23 to 1.42 times less thread CPU** when it keeps every entity the two-decision flow writes except the second action leaf, and **1.42 to 1.66 times less** when it also leaves out the attempt leaf of a first attempt that settled (variant LEAN, the recommended one). Trie branch hashes per trade fall from 167.6 to 127.4 (FULL) and 110.2 (LEAN), store puts from 11 to 10 and 9, decisions-topic bytes by 43 percent, Postgres projection time per trade by 1.11 to 1.26 times. The 1.8 times end of the hypothesis is reached only by also folding the execution and obligation leaves into one (variant FOLDED, 1.43 to 2.0 times, noisy), which changes the entity model and is not recommended. Recommendation: **adopt with changes** (LEAN, as a new capacity profile label), gated on a real-stack run and four owner decisions (section 9). It does not by itself deliver one hot instrument at 10,000 trades/s; it is one of the levers that compose with the others.

## 1. Specification of the combined decision

Prototype: `CombinedCapacityLedger` and `CaptureSettleInput` in `services/platform-runtime/src/test/kotlin/com/reef/platform/calcify/finance/CombinedDecisionPrototype.kt` (test source set, so it ships nowhere). It contains copies of `CapacityLedger`'s Fund, Capture and Settle paths; `CombinedDecisionTest` checks them against the original. (The prototype counts a settled-at-birth first attempt against the attempt budget with an in-memory counter; the recommended rule in section 9, decision 3, counts attempt leaves only, which the existing restore derives without a scan.)

### Input
`CaptureSettleInput(action, execution, buyer, seller, quantity, priceNanos)`: exactly the fields of `Capture`. The first settlement attempt is implied: authority OPERATOR, attempt 1. Canonical bytes are `Capture`'s with the body tag `CAPTURE_SETTLE`, so a combined input never hashes like a capture input (tested). Only the capture source may produce it (the decoder); a combined body on the CONTROL topic is Bad, as a Capture body is today.

### Action key and idempotency
`ActionKey("calcify-finance", "capture-settle", <executionId>)`: one key, one K leaf. A new domain, not the old `capture` domain, so that a ledger holding two-decision history that sees a combined redelivery of an already booked execution answers `EXECUTION_ALREADY_CAPTURED` (in `BENIGN_REFUSALS`) instead of `ACTION_CONFLICT` (which would stop the lane). Same key and same digest: `PRIOR_RESULT` (benign, as today). Same key and different digest (the same `executionId` with other economics): `ACTION_CONFLICT`, as today (the capture key is also derived from the execution id alone today, so this quirk is unchanged). Same execution key under a different action key and equal economics: `EXECUTION_ALREADY_CAPTURED`; different economics: `EXECUTION_CONFLICT`. All tested.

### Decision order
1. Action key present: `PRIOR_RESULT` or `ACTION_CONFLICT` (refused, nothing recorded).
2. Capture checks in the capture path's order: `UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT`, `INVALID_INPUT` (instrument not in the set, buyer equals seller, quantity not positive, negative price), `AMOUNT_OVERFLOW`, `EXECUTION_ALREADY_CAPTURED` or `EXECUTION_CONFLICT`. Any of these refuses the whole trade; nothing is recorded (as for a refused Capture today, after which the lane stops under `failOnBadCapture` unless benign).
3. Budgets: captures, obligations and settle attempts are all checked **before anything is written**; any exhausted gives `BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, refused whole.
4. The settle checks run against the state a capture would leave (a capture touches no position): cash of the buyer below the amount gives `INSUFFICIENT_CASH`, else shares of the seller below the quantity gives `INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`.
5. No shortfall: the four position legs are planned (zero legs dropped, exact arithmetic); overflow gives `BALANCE_OVERFLOW`; otherwise they apply.

### Recorded dispositions: one decision is enough, no new enum values
A recorded combined decision carries the **settle outcome**: `SETTLED` (four legs), `INSUFFICIENT_CASH`, `INSUFFICIENT_SHARES` or `BALANCE_OVERFLOW` (no legs). That the capture succeeded is implied by the input type plus "recorded", because every capture failure is a refusal, never a record. So capture-ok plus settle-refused is one decision, and the existing `FinanceDispositionV1` values suffice. The cost is that a disposition means "the settle outcome of a capture-and-settle" for that input type. The alternative (composite values `CAPTURED_SETTLED`, `CAPTURED_INSUFFICIENT_CASH`, ...) is self-describing but takes four new enum numbers (19 and up) for no behavioural gain; it is a taste decision for the owner.

### Entities written
| Flow | New leaves per trade | Updated leaves | Total leaf updates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two decisions (today) | X, O, A(1), K(capture), K(settle) = 5 | 4 positions (two may be new), O rewritten by the settle | 10 (O twice) |
| Combined FULL | X, O (written once, final), A(1), K = 4 | 4 positions | 8 |
| Combined LEAN (recommended) | X, O, K = 3 on success; X, O, A(1), K on a shortfall | 4 positions | 7 on success |
| Combined FOLDED (limit only) | X' (execution and obligation in one leaf), K = 2 | 4 positions | 6 |

LEAN reasoning: for an obligation that settled in its first attempt, the attempt leaf `A(exec,1)=SETTLED` carries no information the rest of the state lacks. A later `Settle(exec, n)` for that execution is refused `ALREADY_SETTLED` **before** the attempt-key check, in the existing `settle()` order, so the leaf is never consulted. When the first attempt does **not** settle, `A(exec,1)=INSUFFICIENT_*` is written, because it is what makes a retry with attempt 1 refuse `PRIOR_ATTEMPT` and numbers the next attempt. The K leaf carries the digest and disposition for replay and idempotency, so nothing needed for replay is lost. The projection still writes the attempt row `(exec, 1, disposition)`, derived from the decision (section 6).

The entity classes (P, X, O, A, K) and their encodings are unchanged, so **the existing `CapacityLedger.restore` and `StateCommitment.entities` restore and verify a combined ledger without modification** (tested for FULL and LEAN, including that a restored ledger decides a later operator retry identically to the live one). FOLDED is not restorable by it.

### A later separate Settle retry
Works against an obligation created by a combined decision. After a shortfall the obligation is PENDING with residuals, `A(exec,1)` is on record, and `Settle(exec, 2)` settles it once funded; `Settle(exec, 1)` gives `PRIOR_ATTEMPT`; after settlement every attempt gives `ALREADY_SETTLED`. Identical to the two-decision flow (tested, live and restored).

### Checksum and chain
The decision checksum layout is unchanged (`CapacityLedger.checksumOf`: sequence, action key, input digest, disposition, legs, prior chain, state digest). The input digest is the combined input's digest. `CapacityChain.verifyLink` accepts combined decisions unmodified (tested on every recorded decision). The state digest commits to the trie root after the decision, as now. What changes is the sequence: it counts booked trades, not inputs, and the invariant `actions.size == sequence` still holds (one K per decision). The chain of a combined lane is **not** comparable with a two-decision chain of the same trades (different entity set, sequence and digests): the configuration label must change (section 9), as the commitment label is bound into `CapacityConfig.digest()`.

## 2. Behavioural differences from the two-decision flow

| Topic | Two decisions | Combined | Who relies on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decisions per booked trade, sequence | 2 | 1 | `sequenceBound` (8M default covers a 5 minute slice at 2 per trade: now twice the slice), throughput tests that assert `funds + 2 * trades`, docs quoting decisions/s |
| Capture recorded, settle refused for budget | capture recorded, trade left pending, then lane stops | whole trade refused, nothing recorded, lane stops | auditor: no half-booked trade; intended |
| Attempt budget accounting | every attempt, including successes | LEAN: attempt leaves only (a settled first attempt consumes none; the capture budget already bounds those one to one); FULL: as before | `maxSettleAttempts` meaning |
| Leaf `A(exec,1)=SETTLED` | present | absent in LEAN (present in FULL); projection row unchanged | auditor reading the state tree directly |
| Action key of the settle | `settle/<exec>/1` leaf exists | none; an operator replay of exactly that key gives `ALREADY_SETTLED`, not `PRIOR_RESULT` | nothing found in repo; both are benign |
| Capture-only state visible | between two decisions, a projector batch can end after the capture, so a PENDING row can be read for a trade that settles an instant later | never: the obligation row is written settled | read model consumers |
| Redelivery | `PRIOR_RESULT` x2 | `PRIOR_RESULT` x1 | none (benign both) |
| Refusals of a capture (unknown account, conflict, overflow) | same | same | none |
| Intermediate commitment | a root after the capture alone | none: the root of the post-trade state only | an auditor wanting the post-capture state: it is derivable by replaying, not committed |
| Decision log shape | CAPTURED then SETTLED/INSUFFICIENT rows | one row, input type `CAPTURE_SETTLE` | `FinanceProjector.Rows.add`, `FinanceWire`, replay tools |
| Dispositions | CAPTURED, SETTLED, ... | CAPTURED never appears in a capacity-instant log | tests asserting it |

What stays identical and is tested by differential: final positions, executions, obligations (after every step of 720 random scripts of 140 steps: 3 variants x fixed and admitted accounts x 120 seeds, with funding, shortfalls, overflow, unknown accounts, redelivery, conflicting redelivery, unauthorized and unknown retries), attempts (modulo the LEAN omission), dispositions per step (a pair of two-flow dispositions maps to the combined one), legs, the combined state digest equal to the reference `StateCommitment` of the combined snapshot after every decision, chain verification, projection tables.

What the finite profile relies on is untouched: `FinanceLedger` and its goldens do not change; the prototype does not touch `FinanceModel.kt`. A real adoption must add the variant to the sealed `FinanceInput`, so the finite ledger's `when` must refuse it (it is capacity-only, source-restricted like Capture).

## 3. Projection mapping
Same tables. For a recorded combined decision `d` with input `i`: one `calcify_finance_decision` row; one obligation row inserted already `SETTLED` with residuals 0 when `d.disposition == SETTLED`, else `PENDING` with `(quantity x price, quantity)`; one attempt row `(exec, 1, d.disposition)` derived from the decision; position deltas from the legs as now. `CombinedDecisionTest.theProjectionTablesAreIdenticalAndOnlyTheDecisionRowsHalve` runs a reducer mirroring `FinanceProjector.Rows.add` for both shapes over random scripts and asserts identical obligation rows, attempt rows and net position deltas. The read store and `FinanceReadStore` queries are unaffected (they read obligation, attempt, position); anything that counts decision rows or assumes two per trade changes.

## 4. Measurements

Harness `CombinedDecisionBenchmarkTest` (`-PresearchTests`, env `CAP_TRADES=150000 CAP_REPS=4`): resolved-context records (the production upstream), 2,000 accounts, 8 instruments, 18,000 funding records, 20,000 warm-up trades, 150,000 measured trades per run, a fresh ledger per run, one discarded pass per mode, modes interleaved in random order per repetition, **two independent invocations (8 repetitions per mode for the modes in both)**. Every mode runs the same record loop, a copy of `CapacityProcessor.process` (decode, decide, entity bytes, forward bytes, one store apply with head and meta) over a counting map store, so only the ledger and decoder differ. Mode REAL runs the actual `CapacityProcessor` on a mock context to check that the copy tracks it: REAL measured 62 to 73 us per trade against the copy's TWO 58 to 68 us (the copy is the cheaper by about 5 percent; ratios are within the noise of the repetitions). CPU ns is the processing thread's `ThreadMXBean` CPU time; allocation is `getThreadAllocatedBytes`. Hashes, puts, bytes and allocation are deterministic and identical across repetitions; CPU and wall time are noisy (rep 1 of the second invocation shows FOLDED at 44 us against 33 us in its other three runs).

| Mode (what it writes) | Branch hashes / trade | Store puts / trade | Put bytes / trade | Decisions and forwards / trade | Forward bytes / trade | Alloc B / trade | CPU us / trade (min..max, 8 reps) | CPU gain, TWO / mode per repetition | Wall trades/s (laptop, contended) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TWO (today) | 167.6 | 11 | 1,772 | 2 | 934 | 57,160 | 58.0..67.5 | 1.00 | 12.6k..16.6k |
| TWOLEAN (today's flow without the settled-attempt leaf, 4 reps) | 149.8 | 10 | 1,638 | 2 | 934 | 53,080 | 51.6..53.1 | 1.10..1.20 | 17.5k..18.5k |
| FULL (one decision, all entities) | 127.4 | 10 | 1,587 | 1 | 528 | 43,859 | 45.3..48.8 | 1.23..1.42 | 16.1k..21.0k |
| LEAN (one decision, no settled attempt leaf) | **110.2** | **9** | 1,453 | 1 | 528 | 40,167 | **38.4..43.8** | **1.42..1.66** | 18.6k..25.0k |
| FOLDED (limit: X and O one leaf) | 92.8 | 8 | 1,338 | 1 | 528 | 36,539 | 32.7..44.3 | 1.43..2.00 | 17.4k..29.5k |

Reading it:
- The gain is mostly state-commitment work: hashes per trade fall 1.32 times (FULL) and 1.52 times (LEAN), CPU 1.23..1.42 and 1.42..1.66. The hash count scales with the number of distinct leaf paths updated (about 15 branch hashes per leaf at this size), plus the saved second root pass over the paths the two decisions share.
- Attribution: dropping only the settled-attempt leaf (TWOLEAN) is worth 1.10 to 1.20 times; merging the decisions on top of that (LEAN against TWOLEAN) another 1.25 to 1.35 times (one root, one checksum, one input digest, one proto, one forward, no second K and no O rewrite). The merge alone, keeping all entities (FULL), gives the 1.23 to 1.42 times.
- The "1.5 to 1.8 times" hypothesis: LEAN meets its lower half (1.42..1.66); the upper half only with FOLDED, a different entity model.
- Wall-clock trades/s is shown for completeness only; it moved by +-20 percent between repetitions of one mode on this machine.
- Not measured: RocksDB and changelog production (puts are counted, not timed: 11 to 9 puts, 1,772 to 1,453 bytes per trade); the real stack (broker, Streams, projector concurrently); the shortfall path's time (it writes the attempt leaf like today and is rare in the profile).

### Downstream
- **Decisions topic:** 2 records and 934 bytes per trade (key plus value) to 1 record and 528 bytes (-43 percent). The combined body would add about 2 to 4 bytes over the stand-in used here (the capture message in the existing `input` field).
- **Changelog (store puts):** 11 to 9 puts per trade with LEAN (10 FULL); bytes 1,772 to 1,453 (-18 percent), 1,587 for FULL.
- **Postgres rows per trade:** decision rows 2 to 1; obligation 1 and attempt 1 unchanged; position upserts unchanged (up to four per trade, aggregated per batch). Statement counts per batch are the same five.
- **Projector SQL time** (`CombinedProjectionSqlTest`, `-PbrokerTests -PresearchTests`, local Postgres 16 container, removed afterwards; the five set-based statements of `FinanceProjector.write` replayed for 100,000 trades in batches of 1,000 trades, three repetitions interleaved, 200 accounts, 64 instruments): 109..122 us per trade with two decision rows against 92..101 us with one; ratio per repetition 1.26, 1.18, 1.11. The projector was the real-stack bottleneck (7.5k trades/s against the lane's 9.5k in the lane-cost note), so this matters as much as the lane.
- **Lane CPU on the droplet shape:** extrapolation, not measured. One hot lane was thread-saturated at 7.1k decisions/s = 3.5k trades/s; a 1.23 to 1.66 times thread saving would put it at roughly 4.3k to 5.8k trades/s, still far below 10k for one instrument. Four uniform lanes (22k decisions/s = 11k trades/s there) would reach roughly 13.5k to 18k trades/s.

## 5. Prior art (brief)
- **TigerBeetle** separates single-phase transfers (execute immediately) from two-phase ones: a pending transfer reserves, and a later post or void resolves it exactly once, referencing the pending transfer by `pending_id`; resolving again returns an error such as already posted. Each transfer `id` is the idempotency key and a retry returns `exists`; a linked chain of events succeeds or fails together. Our instant profile has no reservation phase to preserve: a combined decision is the single-phase case (a trade moves cash and shares at once), and the PENDING obligation that remains after a shortfall is not a reservation (it holds nothing) but a retryable claim, so two-phase machinery is not the model to copy. TigerBeetle's linked chain is the closest analogue to what the two-decision flow already gets from one Streams transaction per record (both decisions commit together or neither); the combined decision gets the same atomicity without needing two records. Sources: [Transfers reference](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/reference/transfer/), [Two-phase transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/two-phase-transfers/), [Reliable transaction submission](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/reliable-transaction-submission/).
- **Clearing and settlement practice.** Securities settlement systems describe three delivery-versus-payment models; the Reserve Bank of Australia's description of Model 1 is final transfer of payment and securities "contemporaneously and trade-by-trade in real time", which is what the instant profile does, while Models 2 and 3 net payments or both. Netting systems (a central counterparty after novation, as NZX Clearing describes) keep capture (novation, confirmation) and settlement as separate steps because settlement is netted and deferred (T+1, T+2); a system that settles gross and instantly has no reason to log them separately. Sources: [RBA, standard 10 (DvP models)](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/financial-market-infrastructure/clearing-and-settlement-facilities/standards/securities-settlement-facilities/2024/standard-10.html), [NZX Clearing, role](https://new.nzx.com/services/nzx-clearing/the-role-of-nzx-clearing). I found no public specification of an exchange that logs a single combined record for both; treat that as absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. The finite profile and the non-instant capacity profile (`autoSettle = false`, netting or deferred settlement later) keep two inputs by design.
- **Idempotency keys for a combined operation.** Stripe's documented behaviour is the pattern we already follow: the key names the logical operation, the first result is stored and replayed, and the same key with different parameters is an error. That maps to action key = one per execution, stored digest and disposition in K, `PRIOR_RESULT` for the same digest and `ACTION_CONFLICT` otherwise. A combined input has one logical operation, so one key (a derived `/1` settle key would only matter if the two halves could be retried independently, which in the combined flow they cannot). Source: [Stripe, idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests).

## 6. Compatibility and migration consequences if adopted
- **Proto (`contracts/proto/calcify_finance.proto`):** a new `FinanceInputV1` body, for example `FinanceCaptureSettleV1 capture_settle = 5 { FinanceCaptureV1 capture = 1; }`. `FinanceWire.fromProto` refuses unknown fields, so **consumers (the projector, replay and evidence tools) must be upgraded before producers**, as the proto's own comment says for new dispositions. No new disposition numbers are needed.
- **Model:** a new sealed `FinanceInput` variant (digest, canonical bytes), handled in `CapacityLedger.decide`; `FinanceLedger` refuses it. `CapacityLedger` gets the `captureAndSettle` path (about 40 lines, the prototype's `decide(CaptureSettleInput)`); the Fund and Settle paths stay.
- **Decoders:** `CapacityResolvedContextDecoder` and `CapacityInputDecoders` emit one item per trade when the plan says so; `CapacityPlan.autoSettle` becomes a mode (`TWO_DECISIONS | COMBINED | NONE`) in the plan JSON and in the lane binding digest. `controlItems` must reject the new body. `BENIGN_REFUSALS` already covers `PRIOR_RESULT` and `EXECUTION_ALREADY_CAPTURED`.
- **Configuration label:** `CapacityConfig.digest()` must change with the mode (and the genesis with it), so a store, log or projection built under one mode is refused by the other. An in-place switch of a live lane is not supported and not needed (profile selection is per run).
- **Projector:** one `when` branch in `Rows.add` (section 3); `FinanceProjectorPostgresTest` and the capacity end-to-end test add the combined shape. No migration: same tables, same constraints (`calcify_finance_attempt` still gets its row).
- **Tests and goldens:** `StateCommitment` goldens and finite chain goldens do not change (entity classes and encodings unchanged). Tests that assume two decisions per trade change only when run in the combined mode: `CapacityLedgerTest` (`2L * accountCount + 2L * 6_000`), `CapacityLaneThroughputTest` (`funds + 2L * trades`, decisions/s), the S0/S4 spike tests, `CapacityEndToEndTest`'s independent replay. The default remains two decisions, so none of them change until the owner picks the mode.
- **Docs:** CAPACITY_PROFILE decision 7 ("the decoder follows every captured execution with its first settlement attempt"), CAPACITY_RUNTIME's `autoSettle`, LANE_COST and HOT_INSTRUMENT (decisions/s become trades/s), THROUGHPUT docs that quote decisions per second.
- **Replay and audit:** the log of a combined lane replays only with the combined input, so replay tooling must know both input kinds. A deterministic replay of the combined log reproduces the same chain (tested: same inputs, same decisions on a fresh ledger).

## 7. What this does not change
- The hot instrument stays one lane thread. Combined decisions lower the per-trade cost, they do not parallelize it.
- It overlaps with the lagged commitment (B1): both cut root recomputation, so their gains do not multiply (B1 was measured at about +11 percent at K=32 on the old flow; on the combined flow there is one root per trade already, so B1's remaining gain is smaller).
- Hash cost remains the largest term: 110 branch hashes per trade even after LEAN.

## 9. Recommendation and owner decisions
**Adopt with changes.** Adopt the LEAN combined decision as a new, explicitly selected capacity mode (not the default until the gate below passes), keep FULL as the conservative variant, and do not pursue FOLDED.

Why: 1.42 to 1.66 times thread CPU per trade and 1.11 to 1.26 times projection time per trade, 43 percent fewer decision bytes, with no change to the entity classes, the commitment, the checksum layout, the restore path or the finite profile, and with the differential test showing equal final state. The cost is a contract change (new input body, mode in the plan and binding, replay and projector support, tests that assume two decisions per trade) and a log that no longer shows capture and settle as two events.

Gate before it becomes the default for any capacity claim: run `CapacityLaneThroughputTest` with the combined flow on the real stack (it needs the projector branch and the processor wiring, not built here) and confirm the end-to-end rate and the independent-replay equality; the no-broker figures above exclude RocksDB, Kafka and Postgres contention.

The owner must decide:
1. **Is one decision per booked trade acceptable as the audit shape** of the instant profile (sequence = trades; no committed post-capture state; a refusal of the settle half refuses the whole trade)? If an external party needs capture and settle as separate committed events, reject and keep two decisions.
2. **LEAN or FULL:** omit the attempt leaf of a settled first attempt (x1.42..1.66) or keep it (x1.23..1.42, state tree identical to today's except one action leaf).
3. **Attempt budget meaning** under LEAN (attempt leaves only, recommended, restore-derivable) versus counting every attempt (needs a scan of K leaves at restore).
4. **Configuration and binding label** for the new mode, and the rollout order (consumers before producers) and that a live lane is not switched in place.
5. **Disposition representation:** reuse the settle dispositions for the combined input (recommended, no enum change) or add composite values.
6. Whether the sequence bound and the throughput contract wording ("decisions per second") are restated in trades per second.
